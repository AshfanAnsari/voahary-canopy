The Stay

Grown around the trees,
not over them.

Voahary Canopy is a village of nine moss-domed pods strung along a waterfall gorge in Madagascar's eastern rainforest — a stay imagined as part of the forest's own architecture. There is no lobby, no desk, no corridor: the walkway is the arrival, the gorge is the view from every room, and the waterfall is the nearest neighbour.

THE WORLD Madagascar Canopy Cascades — an unbuilt architectural concept by Ferial Gharegozlou & Reihan Rahimi Ajdadi (2023). Every image on this page is a render of that concept — drawn, not photographed. We think it deserves to exist.

The Village

Nine pods, one gorge.

Curved walkways run from pod to pod at canopy height; below them, the gorge falls away to a dark pool. Every room looks at the same waterfall — and no two rooms look at it the same way.

The Pillars

Pillars of the concept

01 / INTEGRATION

The forest was here first

Pod platforms are tuned to the trees they stand among, and the architecture follows the slope — the canopy keeps its shape, and the village borrows it.

02 / SUSTAINABILITY

Roofs that breathe

Moss roofs insulate and slow the rain; open walls and woven screens move the air. The concept is drawn to sit lightly — low footprint, local material, no clearing.

03 / CRAFT

Woven by hand

Bamboo lattice, rattan weave, carved door panels and branch-stilt frames — the pods are a showcase of Malagasy making, inside and out.

Experiences

Days, as imagined

No itinerary exists — but if the pods were real, this is what a day would hold.

01

The canopy walk

Dawn on the walkway, mist still in the gorge. Lemurs in the high branches, hornbills crossing the falls, coffee carried out to the rail.

02

The gorge pool

Down the long stair to the dark pool at the waterfall's foot — cold water, fern shade, and nowhere else to be until lunch.

03

The falls at noon

The gorge is a natural amphitheatre: spray on the terraces, rainbows in the fall line, the whole village quiet in the heat.

04

Night canopy

Lamps lit along the walkway, frogs in the dark below, and the moss roofs ticking softly as they cool. The forest does the night shift.
