The Stay

Grown around the
trees, not over them.

Voahary Canopy is a village of nine moss-domed pods strung along a waterfall gorge in Madagascar's eastern rainforest — a stay imagined as part of the forest's own architecture. Curved walkways run from pod to pod at canopy height; below them, the gorge falls away to a dark pool.

There is no lobby, no desk, no corridor. The walkway is the arrival, the gorge is the view from every room, and the waterfall is the nearest neighbour.

An honest note on what you're looking at. Voahary is a design study built inside the world of Madagascar Canopy Cascades, an unbuilt architectural concept by Ferial Gharegozlou & Reihan Rahimi Ajdadi (2023). Every image on this page is a render from that concept — drawn, not photographed. We think it deserves to exist.
01

Integration

Pods rest on branching stilts that mimic the trees they stand among. Nothing is cleared, nothing is flattened — the forest was here first.

02

Sustainability

Living moss roofs insulate the pods, breathe with the canopy, and hand the rain back to the forest that caught it.

03

Craft

Woven bamboo, carved wood and rattan — Malagasy craft traditions carried into every wall, railing and door.

Experiences

Days, as imagined

01

The canopy walk

Out at first light, along the walkways between the pods — the forest wakes up one layer at a time, and the gorge is still holding its mist.

02

The gorge pool

Down the long stair to the pool at the foot of the falls. Cold, dark, shaded at noon — and worth every step back up.

03

Dawn mist

Before the sun clears the ridge, the gorge fills with mist and the whole village floats — the terrace, a coffee, and nowhere to be.

04

Night canopy

After dark the walkways run on lamp-glow, the interiors turn amber, and the forest turns its volume all the way up.
